## 1. NSE access reused

- The repo has no session or cookie client for NSE's `/api/` JSON. It has plain GETs:
  - `HttpFeedFetcher` sends only `User-Agent: Mozilla/5.0` (`backend/app/hat/data/announcements.py:346`, `:370`).
  - `http_fetcher` sends `BROWSER_HEADERS` (`backend/app/hat/data/bhavcopy.py:385`).
- The probe reuses three pieces of that code:
  - `BROWSER_HEADERS` (`bhavcopy.py:45`), with `Accept` switched to JSON;
  - `DEFAULT_INTERVAL_SECONDS = 1.0`, one request a second (`bhavcopy.py:42`);
  - the data layer's `Pacer` (`backend/app/hat/data/pacing.py:57`), in `Client.__init__`
    (`scripts/research/s2_results_probe.py:85`).
- Added, because `/api/` needs cookies: one `requests.Session` that first loads the referring
  page (`s2_results_probe.py:125`).
- On 401/403/429 the client retries after 5, 20 and 60 s, and on 401/403 it re-warms the
  session first (`:53-54`, `:103`). Answers with those statuses and network errors are not
  cached, so a rerun asks for them again.
- `robots.txt` (`cache-dir/robots.txt`, 200) reads `User-agent: * Allow: /` and disallows only
  `/market-data-test`. `/api/` is allowed.

## 5. Filing time vs the results announcement (20 filings)

**How the sample was drawn.** `pick_timing_filings` takes, for the i-th symbol, its
earliest-broadcast filing of the quarter ending in year 2019 + i % 8, quarter i % 4 + 1. If the
company has no filing for that quarter, it takes the nearest quarter. This covers every year
2019–2026, every quarter, and both listings.

**How the announcement was matched.** `timing_rows` searches the announcements from two days
before to one day after the earlier of the listing time and the XBRL name stamp.
`results_announcement` then picks:
- the earliest `Financial Result Updates` item;
- otherwise the earliest `Outcome of Board Meeting` item. In the Integrated Filing era the
  results PDF goes out under that subject, and all 4 matched have text like "has submitted to the
  Exchange, the financial results" (`timing.csv` `announcement_text`).

Matched: 18 of 20. Not matched:
- FEDERALBNK Mar 2019: the announcements API returns 1 unrelated item for the window.
- HDFCLIFE Jun 2024: the insurance listing's earliest row is a 04‑Oct‑2024 re-filing and the
  window holds no announcements.

| Statistic (listing time − announcement) | n | median | min | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All 18 matched | 18 | 4 h 53 m (17,610 s) | 31 m (1,889 s) | 63 days (5,443,768 s) |
| Excluding 3 re-broadcast listings (below) | 15 | 2 h 57 m (10,594 s) | 31 m | 22 h 32 m (81,101 s) |

- **No filing has the listing time or XBRL time before the announcement**
  (`summary.json` `listing_before_announcement` = 0).
- **The XBRL name stamp, read as PM, is never before the announcement either**
  (`stamp_pm_minus_announcement_s`, min 1,889 s).
- **The PDF announcement always comes first**, by 31 minutes to 22.5 hours. Examples:
  - HDFCBANK Jun 2024: announced Sat 20‑Jul 14:15, XBRL Sun 21‑Jul 12:46 (+22.5 h);
  - LT Mar 2023 (+20.8 h) and SONATSOFTW Dec 2022 (+20.1 h) also filed their XBRL the next day.
- **So `public_at` must come from the announcement.** The XBRL time alone would date results up
  to a day late, past a whole session.

## 6. Blocking and data-quality issues

1. **The listing time is not the filing time for 2017–2021.** It is often a later re-broadcast.
   Compare `broadCastDate` with the XBRL file name's date stamp (`listing_vs_stamp.csv`), for
   filings where the listing is more than a day later:

   | Year | Re-broadcast / with stamp | Max lag |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 2017 | 5 / 5 | 23 days |
   | 2018 | 83 / 105 | 71 days |
   | 2019 | 60 / 141 | 115 days |
   | 2020 | 73 / 184 | 96 days |
   | 2021 | 2 / 179 | 372 days |
   | 2022–2026 | 0 | 0 |

   Examples:
   - RELIANCE Mar 2019: listed 06‑May‑2019, stamped 19‑Apr‑2019, announced 18‑Apr‑2019 18:15.
   - BAJFINANCE Jun 2020: listed 18‑Sep‑2020, stamped 21‑Jul‑2020, announced 21‑Jul‑2020 12:05.
   - TATAELXSI Mar 2019: listed 26‑Jun‑2019, announced 24‑Apr‑2019.

   A1 cannot use `broadCastDate` as `xbrl_at` or `public_at` before 2022 without this check.
2. **The XBRL file name stamp uses a 12-hour clock with no AM/PM.** For example,
   `…_17072026075004_WEB.xml` was broadcast 17‑Jul‑2026 19:50:04 (`xbrl_name_stamp`, and the
   test `test_results_probe__xbrl_name_stamp_and_quarters`). In the timing sample, 13 filings
   broadcast after noon carry a stamp that is 12 h early. Use the stamp's date only, never its
   time.
3. **The same XBRL is listed twice under different `seqNumber`s.** This happens 16 to 38 times a
   year from 2018 to 2024 (`listing_vs_stamp.csv` `xbrl_listed_twice`), and never in the
   integrated listing. The `period=Annual` rows from 2018 on mostly repeat the Q4 document: 220
   share the XBRL URL of a Quarterly row, 15 have their own, 21 have none. A1 has to deduplicate
   by XBRL URL or sha256.
4. **Listing gaps:** Jun 2022 for 5 of 19 symbols, plus the one-off holes listed in §4. The
   results did happen (SBIN above), so A1 needs a second source for missing quarters (the
   announcement PDF, or BSE's XBRL) or must accept the holes and flag them.
5. **The announcements API answered `[]` for a 21-day window.** For example, SBIN 25‑Jul to
   15‑Aug‑2022 (`cache:d1/d17eeb815d679da9a4ad282e`), while a 4-day window inside it holds the
   items. Query it in short windows (4 days worked every time).
6. **One announcement time is suspect.** PERSISTENT's 24‑Apr‑2025 board outcome is stamped
   05:08 and its press release 05:16, yet the con-call presentation is at 17:56 and the XBRL at
   20:32. This looks like the same 12-hour fault in `an_dt` for that company. It is a single
   case; A1 should check an announcement before 09:15 against the XBRL time.
7. **No anti-bot or rate-limit blocking** at one request a second: 1,631 requests, no
   401/403/429. The session cookies from one page load lasted the whole ~35-minute run.
8. **Taxonomy:** no gaps. Every downloaded document fell into a known format: no `other`, no
   `not_xbrl`. The `integrated_otherthanbank` namespace appears on 2 LT Mar 2025 documents.

## 7. Recommendation for A1

- **Endpoints:**
  - `corporates-financial-results` (`index=equities`, `period=Quarterly`) for the Reg 33 era up
    to the Dec 2024 quarter;
  - `integrated-filing-results` (`type=Integrated Filing- Financials`) from the Mar 2025 quarter
    on. The two overlap on no period: Dec 2024 is Reg 33 only and Mar 2025 Integrated only, 43
    rows in `documents.csv`;
  - `index=insurance` only to write the insurers' `insurance_skipped` rows;
  - `corporate-announcements` in windows of a few days, for `public_at`.
- **Years:**
  - **Usable from 2020.** XBRL is complete or near-complete. 2020–2021 need `xbrl_at` from the
    XBRL stamp date and from the announcement, not from `broadCastDate`.
  - **2018–2019: partial.** About 80% of symbol-quarters; NBFCs are the worst in mid-2019.
  - **2016–2017:** no XBRL, but the HTML results pages carry the same core figures. If growth
    signals are wanted for 2017–2019 (sales_yoy needs the year before), the HTML pages are the
    only source. Parsing them would be a scope decision for the owner; the plan's A1/A2 assume
    XBRL.
  - **The EP evidence window starts in 2022** (Part C, 5-minute bars). Year-on-year growth for
    2022 needs 2021 quarters, which are covered. So the S2 work needs 2021 onward, and that span
    is usable apart from the listing gaps above.
- **`public_at`:** take the results announcement time, whether `Financial Result Updates` or
  `Outcome of Board Meeting` (Integrated era). The XBRL is 31 min to 22.5 h later in every case
  sampled.
- **Classification:** `format` comes straight from the listing marker or the document
  namespace, and both agree.
